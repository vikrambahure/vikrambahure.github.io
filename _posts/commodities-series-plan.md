**Status:** Planning

---

## The Master Flowchart (post 0 or intro visual)

The single most useful thing for a reader is understanding the production chain.
Every post links back to this map.

```
UPSTREAM EXTRACTION
│
├── CRUDE OIL (wellhead)
│   └── REFINERY
│       ├── LPG (propane + butane) ←────────────────┐
│       ├── Naphtha (lightest liquid fraction)       │
│       ├── Petrol / Gasoline                        │
│       ├── Jet Fuel / ATF                           │
│       ├── Diesel / Gasoil                          │
│       └── Fuel Oil / HSFO (heaviest)               │
│                                                    │
├── NATURAL GAS (wellhead / associated gas)          │
│   ├── Pipeline Gas (methane, regional)             │
│   ├── LNG (same methane, cooled -162°C, shipped)   │
│   └── LPG stripped at gas plant ───────────────────┘
│       (propane + butane removed before pipeline)
│
└── NAPHTHA → STEAM CRACKER (petrochemical plant)
    ├── Ethylene → Polyethylene (plastic bags, pipes)
    │             MEG (+ PTA → Polyester / PET bottles)
    ├── Propylene → Polypropylene (packaging film, auto parts)
    └── BTX (Benzene, Toluene, Paraxylene)
        └── Paraxylene → PTA → Polyester fibre / PET
```

**Key distinctions to call out in the intro:**
- LPG ≠ LNG. LPG = propane + butane (from crude refining AND gas processing). LNG = liquefied methane. Completely different molecules, uses, supply chains.
- Natural gas and LNG are the same molecule. LNG is just the shipping form — cooled to -162°C to reduce volume 600x, shipped in tankers, regasified at destination.
- Naphtha has two lives: fuel blending (minor) and petrochemical feedstock (major). The feedstock route is what connects an oil shock to your PET bottle and polyester shirt.

## Post List

### Post 1 — Crude Oil + Benchmarks
**The aha:** Three prices exist because three different markets need different references.

**Benchmarks:**
- **Brent** (ICE, London): North Sea light sweet crude. Prices ~70% of world's oil. All Middle East exports to Europe and Asia are priced as Brent ± differential. The global benchmark.
- **WTI** (NYMEX, New York): West Texas Intermediate. US domestic crude. Delivery at Cushing, Oklahoma (landlocked). US refinery benchmark. Often trades at discount to Brent when pipelines are full.
- **Dubai/Oman** (DME, Dubai): Middle East medium sour crude. Asia-Pacific benchmark. Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq) price Asian sales off Dubai.
- **OPEC Basket:** Average of member country crude blends. Used for OPEC policy reference, not traded directly.
- **Platts/Argus:** Price assessment agencies — not exchanges. They survey traders and publish daily spot price assessments. Most physical crude trades reference Platts Dated Brent.

**India uses:** Dubai/Oman for Gulf purchases (majority), Brent for non-Gulf
**UK uses:** Brent (it's North Sea crude — literally produced there)

### Post 2 — Natural Gas + LNG
**The aha:** Same molecule. Two completely different supply chains — one regional, one global.

**Benchmarks:**
- **Henry Hub** (NYMEX): US natural gas hub in Louisiana. US domestic benchmark.
- **TTF** (ICE): Title Transfer Facility, Netherlands. European gas benchmark. Most European gas contracts reference TTF.
- **NBP** (ICE): National Balancing Point, UK. UK domestic gas price.
- **JKM** (Platts): Japan-Korea Marker. LNG spot price for Asia-Pacific. The premium LNG benchmark — Asia pays most.
- **LNG contracts:** Long-term contracts (15-20yr) historically oil-indexed (priced as % of JCC — Japan Customs-Cleared crude). Spot market now growing via JKM.

### Post 3 — LPG (NOT LNG)
**The aha:** LPG = propane + butane. It comes from two separate sources — crude refining and gas processing. It is NOT natural gas.

**Benchmarks:**
- **Saudi Aramco CP (Contract Price):** Monthly price set by Saudi Aramco for propane and butane. Most Asian LPG contracts reference Saudi CP. Released around 25th of the month for the following month.
- **Mont Belvieu** (OPIS): US LPG hub in Texas. US domestic propane/butane price.
- **Argus Far East Index (AFEI):** Spot LPG prices for Asia.

**Two sources of LPG:**
1. Refinery LPG: stripped from crude during distillation (~40% of global supply)
2. Gas plant LPG: propane and butane are removed from natural gas before it enters pipelines (~60% of global supply). Middle East gas plants are the dominant source.

**Uses of LPG:**
- Cooking fuel (India's Ujjwala scheme — 300mn households)
- Heating (Europe, Japan)
- Autogas (vehicle fuel in some markets)
- Petrochemical feedstock (propane dehydrogenation → propylene)

**Top 5 LPG exporters 2024:**
| Country | % |
|---|---|
| USA | ~27% |
| Saudi Arabia | ~14% |
| Qatar | ~8% |
| Australia | ~7% |
| UAE | ~6% |

**India LPG supply mix (2024):**
| Source | % |
|---|---|
| Middle East (Saudi, UAE, Qatar) | ~55% |
| Domestic (ONGC/refinery) | ~35% |
| USA | ~8% |
| Others | ~2% |

**UK LPG (smaller market — mainly rural heating):**
Largely domestic refinery supply + Norwegian imports.

**Surprising number:** India is the world's largest LPG importer. 99% of Indian households now have an LPG connection (Ujjwala), up from 55% in 2014.

### Post 4 — Diesel / Gasoil
**The aha:** Diesel is the economy's circulatory system. Every truck, tractor, train, and construction site runs on it.

**Benchmarks:**
- **ICE Gasoil** (ICE London): European/global diesel benchmark. 0.1% sulphur.
- **ULSD** (NYMEX): Ultra-Low Sulphur Diesel, US benchmark.
- **Singapore Gasoil:** Asia benchmark (Platts assessed).
- Crack spread: Diesel price minus crude price = refinery margin on diesel. Widening crack = tight diesel supply.

**Who consumes diesel:**
- Road freight (trucks): ~40% of global diesel
- Agriculture (tractors, irrigation pumps): ~15%
- Industrial/mining: ~20%
- Rail: ~10%
- Marine/power: ~15%

**India note:** Diesel is the single most consumed petroleum product in India (~40% of total product consumption). Bulk diesel for trucks and agriculture is market-priced. Retail diesel at the pump has been deregulated since 2014 but OMCs still delay pass-throughs politically.

**UK note:** Heavy diesel use in heating oil (red diesel) for off-road and agricultural use.

---

### Post 5 — Petrol / Gasoline
**The aha:** Petrol matters less for India than diesel because car penetration is lower and two-wheelers dominate.

**Benchmarks:**
- **RBOB** (NYMEX): Reformulated Blendstock for Oxygenate Blending. US gasoline benchmark.
- **Eurobob** (Platts): European gasoline.
- **Singapore Mogas 92/95** (Platts): Asia benchmark.

**India:** Retail petrol is politically sensitive. Government uses excise duty cuts as a buffer (₹8-10/litre cuts seen in 2022 to absorb crude spike). Two-wheelers (~200mn) are the primary petrol consumers, not cars.

### Post 6 — Naphtha
**The aha:** Naphtha is where crude oil becomes plastic, polyester, fertiliser, and paint.

**Benchmarks:**
- **MOPJ** (Platts): Mean of Platts Japan. The Asian naphtha benchmark. Most Asian crackers use MOPJ for feedstock pricing.
- **CIF NWE** (Platts): Cost-insurance-freight Northwest Europe. European naphtha benchmark.

**Two uses:**
1. **Gasoline blending** (~30%): added to petrol pool
2. **Steam cracking** (~70%): fed into crackers → ethylene, propylene, BTX

**Key chain:** Naphtha → cracker → paraxylene → PTA (purified terephthalic acid) + MEG (monoethylene glycol) → polyester fibre / PET resin

PTA is ~80% crude-derived via naphtha → paraxylene route. This is why a Hormuz disruption at +20% crude flows directly into Indian textile and packaging costs.

**India:** India is both an importer and exporter of naphtha. Large refineries (Reliance, IOCL) export surplus naphtha; petrochemical plants import to supplement. Net: marginal importer. Priced off MOPJ.

---

### Post 7 — Jet Fuel / ATF
**The aha:** Airlines are a leveraged oil trade. Fuel = 25-35% of operating costs.

**Benchmarks:**
- **Jet CIF NWE** (Platts): European jet benchmark.
- **Jet Kerosene Singapore** (Platts): Asian benchmark.
- ATF (Aviation Turbine Fuel): India-specific term. Taxed differently from diesel/petrol. State governments add their own levies (wide variation across Indian states).

**India quirk:** ATF in India is not under GST — it's a state subject. A flight refuelling in Maharashtra pays different ATF price than one in Andhra Pradesh. This is a structural inefficiency that airlines lobby to fix every year.

---

### Post 8 — Fuel Oil / HSFO
**The aha:** The bottom of the barrel — literally. The heaviest fraction. Used in shipping and power. The IMO 2020 sulphur cap created a major price dislocation.

**Benchmarks:**
- **HSFO 380 Singapore** (Platts): High Sulphur Fuel Oil, the traditional bunker fuel.
- **VLSFO 0.5% Singapore** (Platts): Very Low Sulphur Fuel Oil — post-IMO 2020 compliant fuel.
- **Hi-5 spread:** VLSFO price minus HSFO price = compliance premium.

**IMO 2020:** From Jan 1, 2020, ships must use <0.5% sulphur fuel (down from 3.5%). This created a structural demand shift from HSFO to VLSFO and scrubber-fitted ships.

---

### Post 9 — The Petrochemical Chain (Ethylene → Polyester)
**The aha:** Your PET water bottle and polyester shirt started as crude oil from the Middle East.

**Chain:**
Crude → Naphtha → Steam cracker → Ethylene + Propylene + BTX
Paraxylene (from BTX) + acetic acid → PTA
Ethylene + oxygen → MEG (monoethylene glycol)
PTA + MEG → PET resin (bottles) / Polyester fibre (clothing)

**Benchmarks:**
- Ethylene: CFR NE Asia (Platts), CE NWE (Platts)
- PTA: CFR China (ICIS)
- MEG: CFR China (ICIS)
- Polyester: local Chinese spot

**India relevance:** India imports PTA (~4mn tonnes/year) and MEG (~3mn tonnes/year) for textile sector. Both are priced off crude via the naphtha → paraxylene route. The Surat textile cluster is the end-consumer. This is the Hormuz → Surat transmission chain.
