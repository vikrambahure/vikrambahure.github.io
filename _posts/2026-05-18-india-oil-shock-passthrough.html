India Oil-Shock Pass-Through

A Leontief Cascade View for a Hormuz Closure

Vikram Bahure · 18 May 2026

Executive Summary

  • View. Two cross-asset calls for an Indian buy-side book under a Hormuz-closure scenario. Underweight INR vs USD: each $10/bbl on Brent widens India's current-account deficit by roughly 0.4% of GDP, with the oil-import bill running $2-4bn/month above pre-war levels. Underweight Indian crude-derivative downstream: fertilisers, synthetic textiles, specialty chemicals, and aviation, where total Leontief multipliers run 1.18-1.44 and input-cost lift sits in the 10-24% range.

  • Methodology. Standard price-Leontief pass-through on the ADB Multi-Region Input-Output table (India, 35-sector). Sector price changes are computed as ΔP = (I - A')⁻¹ · s_crude · Δp_crude, where A is the matrix of technical coefficients, s_crude is each sector's direct crude input share, and Δp_crude is the Brent shock. The Leontief inverse captures direct plus all rounds of indirect intermediate-input price propagation; the current-account impact is sized via standard oil-import elasticity.

  • Headline result. Brent settling at $90-100/bbl (from a $67 pre-war floor) drives total IO multipliers of 1.44 (Fertilisers), 1.38 (Synthetic Textiles), 1.35 (Specialty Chemicals), 1.18 (Aviation). Pass-through into sector input costs runs from +14% (Aviation op cost) to +24% (Fertilisers, $100/bbl). The current-account deficit widens by roughly 1% of GDP.

  • Sector dispersion. The shock travels through two distinct channels. Direct: naphtha and ATF are crude derivatives, so Fertilisers and Aviation absorb the move within one quarter. Indirect: petrochemical feedstock propagates through intermediate-input chains into Synthetic Textiles and Specialty Chemicals over 1-3 quarters, with pricing-power asymmetry determining who passes through to consumers and who absorbs into margin.

  • The honest gap. Cost-push only. There is no FX-feedback channel into CPI pass-through, no demand-side macro model, and no second-round wage-price interaction. The rates leg is deliberately omitted (Indian government bond liquidity is outside my current working set). Sector coverage is limited to the four with computed Leontief multipliers in the underlying analysis; no overweight recommendations are offered because computing multipliers for the "oil-light" beneficiaries (IT services, private banks) is scoped but not yet executed. Magnitudes are taken from a single-vintage ADB MRIO table without rolling robustness checks.
